The Problem

The AI race is on. Most enterprises are already losing.

In 2026, AI is no longer a pilot program. It is a board-level accountability. Yet the gap between enterprise AI spending and enterprise AI outcomes has never been wider.

Investments are climbing. Proof of ROI is scarce. The same boardrooms that approved seven-figure AI budgets are now asking uncomfortable questions — and the answers are being dressed up rather than diagnosed.

This isn’t a technology problem. It’s five structural blindspots that quietly stall even the most resource-rich AI strategies. And most leadership teams have never had the honest conversation to name them.

Why This Moment Is Different

2026 is not the year to still be learning on the job.

The competitors who figured this out 18 months ago are now compounding their advantage. Agentic AI has changed the cost model. Regulatory pressure has changed the risk model. And the workforce’s patience for performative AI strategies is at a breaking point. The window to course-correct — without losing ground — is now.

The Compounding Gap

Every quarter your AI governance lacks structure, the cost of correction grows. Organizations that acted in 2024 now operate at fundamentally lower cost bases than those still running ungoverned pilots.

Agentic AI Changed Everything

Token costs, attack surfaces, and governance failures multiply when agents start coordinating. The IT cost model from 2023 is obsolete. Most enterprises don’t yet have a replacement.

The People Problem Is Getting Worse

Workforce resistance isn’t softening. It’s hardening into sabotage. The organizations closing the adoption gap are doing so through culture architecture, not tool mandates.

Regulatory Pressure Arrived

EU AI Act enforcement, SEC disclosure requirements, and industry-specific frameworks are now real. Ungoverned AI deployments are a compliance liability, not just an operational one.

Board Scrutiny Is Intensifying

Boards are now asking for AI ROI attribution, not AI roadmaps. The era of buying time with strategy decks and pilot announcements is ending. Results are the only currency that matters now.

Pilot-to-Production Is Still Broken

The capability to run a successful proof-of-concept has never been higher. The capability to scale it to production has not kept pace. Most AI initiatives still die in the handoff.

Blindspot 03

Ungoverned AI Sprawl

80% of employees use unapproved AI tools at work.

45% report confirmed or suspected data leaks from unauthorized AI — and most organizations couldn’t pull the plug on a rogue agent if they had to. Shadow AI is not a future risk. It is an active, ongoing condition in virtually every mid-to-large enterprise today. This panel moves past the handbrake governance model and gives you the architecture for speed with guardrails.

  • Defining shadow AI: where governance breaks down and who owns it
  • Building a “speed with guardrails” model — governance that enables, not blocks
  • Agentic AI attack surfaces: why autonomous agents dramatically increase exposure
  • Multi-tenant governance architecture for distributed organizations

29% of employees admit to actively sabotaging AI strategy.

They’re winning with deliberate change management, psychological safety, and a workforce architecture built for transformation at scale.

  • Building AI literacy across non-technical teams without triggering fear or resistance
  • Change management playbooks for agentic AI rollouts at enterprise scale
  • The “AI Elite” trap: why empowering superstars creates organizational fragility
  • Trust, resistance, and the psychology of adoption — what the data actually shows
